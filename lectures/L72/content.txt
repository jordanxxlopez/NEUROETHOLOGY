Contingent stimulation trains a neural response|shahaf|s6|6|Response acquisition with and without contingent stimulus removal

The authors analyze possible conditions for artificial suffering, including subjective experience, a self-model, and negatively valued states. These are elements of their ethical framework, rather than physiological outcomes measured by the Pong experiment.

Precaution informs choices under uncertainty|ethics|text||
The **precautionary principle** supports protective action when an uncertain risk could have serious consequences. Milford and colleagues apply it to the possibility that increasingly complex synthetic neural systems could develop morally relevant experiences. Their proposal is an ethical position grounded in a particular account of consciousness.
They recommend limits on the number and types of neurons used, the experimental applications, and research duration while criteria for experience and suffering are developed. They also call for combined philosophical and empirical work on definitions and measurements of consciousness and sentience.
These recommendations concern how research should be governed under uncertainty. They differ from an experimentally established welfare threshold. The connection to neurobiology lies in which properties of a neural preparation are considered relevant to experience, how those properties are assessed, and how experimental design changes in response to the resulting ethical judgment.

Doom is a reported extension of embodiment|framework,pong|f1c|1C|Published comparison of virtual and physical embodiments
Sellar and Rouleau's 2026 perspective describes virtual embodiment as one route for synthetic biological intelligence. **Embodiment** here means a causal connection between a neural construct and an environment through input, action, and feedback. Their discussion mentions Doom as a later extension of game interaction.
The cited experimental Pong work supplies documented encoding, decoding, feedback schedules, and performance comparisons. The perspective's Doom mention supplies no corresponding experimental protocol or measured learning trajectory. The available sources therefore support different levels of detail for the two games.
A game connection and adaptive task performance are separate achievements. The former establishes that neural output can affect an environment; the latter requires measured improvement and appropriate controls. The distinction determines whether a claim concerns a functioning interface, a changing action policy, or a demonstrated learning effect under specified conditions.

The interface defines sensing and action|framework,pong,brainoware|f1a|1A|Published sensor-controller-actuator feedback loop
**Cybernetics** studies control and communication in systems that link sensing, action, and feedback. Sellar and Rouleau describe controllers whose outputs alter the environment and thereby change subsequent input. The functional loop can include both living neural tissue and electronic components.
Pong supplied a small action space, upward or downward paddle movement, and electrically encoded ball information. Brainoware supplied classification and prediction readouts. These systems assigned different meanings to neural output because their interfaces implemented different computational tasks and different relationships between input and consequence.
Adaptive behavior is consequently a property of the coupled arrangement. Encoding determines accessible information, decoding determines available output, and feedback determines the consequences returned to the network. Evaluating performance or welfare requires specifying those relationships alongside the neural preparation, because changing the interface changes what the same tissue can sense and control.
