# Vickers & Baker (1994): verified experimental evidence

Reference: Vickers NJ, Baker TC (1994). Reiterative responses to single strands of odor promote sustained upwind flight and odor source location by moths. PNAS 91(13):5756–5760. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.91.13.5756

## Preparation and methods

Flying male tobacco budworms, *Heliothis virescens*, were exposed to a six-component species-specific pheromone blend in a wind tunnel. Wind speed remained 0.6 m/s across the experiments. An air-pulsing device delivered 20-ms pulses at 1, 2, 4, 5 or 10 pulses/s; air flow through the device was held at 5 mL/s. Video recordings measured flight trajectories. Separate single-filament experiments synchronized odor passage with calibrated sequential lights moving at wind speed.

The freely flying electroantennogram preparation used an excised third antenna mounted on the moth's thorax, with both of the moth's own antennae intact. Its voltage recorded local odor contacts, rather than the animal's brain activity. Video and electroantennogram records were synchronized within 0.01 s.

## Findings that can be taught quantitatively

- A single intercepted pheromone strand evoked an average upwind surge of 13.4 cm lasting 0.38 s. Surge onset followed odor contact by an average 0.30 s.
- No-filament control trials produced no upwind displacement by casting males.
- Pulse rates below 4 filaments/s activated moths and elicited casting but did not sustain upwind flight under the tested conditions.
- At 4 filaments/s, trajectories included substantial crosswind casting; at 10 filaments/s, trajectories were straighter and directed farther upwind. The 5-filaments/s condition was intermediate.
- Wind speed was held constant, excluding a change in wind velocity as the explanation for the between-rate difference. Pulse-rate treatments also change odor delivery over time; they do not establish that temporal rate is independent of every integrated exposure variable.
- During free flight in a normal point-source plume, casting began on average 0.30 s after the last recorded filament contact. Upwind flights involved more frequent contacts than crosswind flights.

## Interpretation and limits

Repeated odor encounters can renew short upwind responses before prolonged clean-air intervals allow casting. This supports a relationship between intermittency and behavioral sequencing. The paper proposes a phasic/tonic explanation, but the experiment does not identify the specific central neurons, synapses, neurotransmitters or ion channels responsible for those states.

The measured latency and surge duration apply to this species and preparation. They must not be assigned to *Bombyx mori* without a separate silkmoth source. Kramer's nonpheromone-attractivity claim is omitted, not replaced by a claim that this study tested the same manipulation.
