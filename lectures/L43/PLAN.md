# Lecture 43 research plan

Axolotl: lateral-line sensing, electroreception, and sensory processing of courtship signals

46-slide deliverable: title, 44 content slides, six exam-level takeaways. Arial throughout; select and record a new permitted theme only when the deck is built.

The evidence will distinguish mechanoreceptive neuromasts, electroreceptive ampullary organs, and chemical courtship cues. Electroreception is established in axolotls; a dedicated electrical courtship signal must not be claimed without direct evidence.

Content allocation after the original PDFs are available:

- 8 content slides: courtship context, lateral-line receptor distribution, neuromast and ampullary anatomy.
- 5: receptor development and sorting of hindbrain afferent projections.
- 7: electric-field responses, stimulus controls, voltage-gated potassium-channel evidence and its limits.
- 6: midbrain representations, thresholds, response timing, directional sensitivity and multimodal alignment.
- 4: efferent activity, conduction and motor-related modulation.
- 6: direct mechanosensory courtship experiments from the explicitly labeled 2026 preprint; distinguish receiver behavior from nerve responses.
- 6: courtship-secreted proteins, gland transcripts, pheromone characterization and evidential limits.
- 2: relationships among modalities and what causal experiments establish.

Each slide will use 3–5 source-based paragraphs, full teaching notes, verified references and original source figures. No numbers, receptor mechanisms or causal claims will be inferred from abstracts alone. These are drafting allocations, not an agenda slide in the deck.
