# Additional ΔFosB teaching slides

## Repeated drug exposure recruits ΔFosB in defined neurons

**ΔFosB** is a shortened protein produced from an alternatively spliced transcript of the FosB gene. It acts as a **transcription factor**, a protein that regulates the expression of other genes. Repeated stimulation can change neuronal responses beyond an individual exposure. Lobo and colleagues measured ΔFosB in identified mouse striatal neurons after repeated drug treatment. Cell labeling distinguished populations mixed together in whole-tissue measurements.

**Medium spiny neurons** are inhibitory projection neurons named for the small protrusions on their dendrites. The **nucleus accumbens**, a ventral part of the striatum, contains core and shell subdivisions. The investigators distinguished neurons expressing D1-type dopamine receptors from those expressing D2-type receptors. Seven daily cocaine treatments increased ΔFosB predominantly in D1-type neurons in the examined striatal regions. Measurement 24 h after treatment separated persistent induction from immediate exposure.

Different substances recruited different cell populations in the same study. Voluntary cocaine self-administration also favored ΔFosB induction in D1-type neurons. Morphine treatment and heroin self-administration recruited both D1-type and D2-type populations. Sucrose exposure and environmental enrichment could also increase ΔFosB in both populations. Gene regulation depended on substance, cell population and exposure conditions.

## Phosphorylation helps ΔFosB resist degradation

**Protein half-life** is the time over which half of a labeled protein population disappears. Ulery and colleagues measured ΔFosB turnover using a pulse of labeled amino acids followed by an unlabeled chase. In PC12 cells, a rat-derived neuron-like preparation, ΔFosB had a half-life of approximately 10 h. Full-length FosB was already known to turn over much faster in cell culture. These measurements concern cellular protein persistence rather than behavioral duration.

**Phosphorylation** adds a phosphate group to a protein and can change its interactions or stability. The investigators identified serine at position 27, termed **Ser27**, as a stabilizing phosphorylation site. Replacing serine with alanine prevented phosphorylation there. In PC12 cells, this substitution shortened the measured half-life from approximately 11 h to approximately 4 h. Mimicking the phosphate’s negative charge instead increased stability.

A **proteasome** is a cellular complex that breaks down selected proteins. Two different proteasome inhibitors reversed the accelerated loss of the alanine-substituted ΔFosB protein. The altered amino acid affected this degradation pathway. The study also identified **casein kinase 2**, an enzyme that transfers phosphate groups, as a regulator of Ser27 phosphorylation. Protein stabilization supplies a molecular mechanism through which repeated activation can sustain a transcriptional response after the initiating stimulus has ended.

Ulery PG, Rudenko G, Nestler EJ. (2006). Regulation of DeltaFosB stability by phosphorylation. The Journal of neuroscience : the official journal of the Society for Neuroscience 26:5131-5142. https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.4970-05.2006

## ΔFosB and CaMKII couple gene regulation to plasticity

**CaMKII** is calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II, an enzyme whose activation links calcium signals to protein phosphorylation. Robison and colleagues examined its relationship with ΔFosB in the nucleus accumbens. Repeated cocaine exposure in rats increased CaMKII particularly in the accumbens shell under the tested conditions. ΔFosB promoted expression of the gene encoding the alpha form of CaMKII in D1-type neurons. This transcriptional relationship connects a persistent nuclear protein with a signaling enzyme that acts within neurons.

The investigators manipulated ΔFosB using viral expression in mouse accumbens neurons. Increasing ΔFosB increased the density of **dendritic spines**, small neuronal protrusions that receive many excitatory synaptic contacts. Coexpressing a peptide inhibitor of CaMKII prevented this structural effect. The manipulation also prevented the ΔFosB-associated enhancement of cocaine locomotion. The interventions connected this pathway to neuronal structure and drug-related behavior.

CaMKII also phosphorylated ΔFosB at Ser27, linking the kinase back to the transcription factor’s stability. The proteins formed a positive regulatory loop in this preparation. **Sensitization** refers here to an increased behavioral response following repeated drug exposure. Cocaine-induced locomotion supplied the behavioral measurement alongside molecular and structural changes. Cell identity and accumbens subregion determine where this particular loop is engaged.

Robison AJ, Vialou V, Mazei-Robison M, Feng J, Kourrich S, Collins M, Wee S, Koob G, Turecki G, Neve R, Thomas M, Nestler EJ. (2013). Behavioral and structural responses to chronic cocaine require a feedforward loop involving ΔFosB and calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II in the nucleus accumbens shell. The Journal of neuroscience : the official journal of the Society for Neuroscience 33:4295-4307. https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.5192-12.2013

## Social stress recruits different ΔFosB cell populations

Lobo and colleagues exposed mice to 10 d of repeated social defeat and then measured their social interaction. **Susceptible** animals displayed reduced interaction under the study’s behavioral classification. **Resilient** animals maintained social interaction despite exposure to the same defeat procedure. Researchers measured ΔFosB in D1-type and D2-type medium spiny neurons separately. Separate measurements identified which population responded.

Susceptible mice had increased ΔFosB in D2-type neurons in accumbens core, accumbens shell and dorsal striatum. Resilient mice instead had increased ΔFosB in D1-type neurons in those regions. Measurement followed defeat and social-interaction assessment. Opposite behavioral outcomes accompanied induction in different populations. Total ΔFosB alone would omit this cell-type distinction.

The study also examined repeated treatment with **fluoxetine**, an antidepressant that inhibits serotonin reuptake. Fourteen days of treatment increased ΔFosB predominantly in D1-type neurons in the examined striatal regions. Cocaine, resilience and antidepressant treatment shared a cell-type pattern under different conditions. ΔFosB is part of experience-dependent gene regulation across rewarding, stressful and pharmacological contexts. The behavioral meaning of its induction depends on the cells and the experience that recruited it.

## Human ΔFosB findings depend on the sampled brain region

Human research compared postmortem tissue from cocaine-dependent and comparison subjects. Robison and colleagues found increased ΔFosB and CaMKII in the sampled human nucleus accumbens. Rodent interventions linked these proteins to cocaine-responsive cellular and behavioral changes. Human measurements identified a corresponding regional molecular association. Postmortem comparisons leave its timing relative to drug use unresolved.

Gajewski and colleagues examined two additional regions: the **hippocampus**, involved in memory processing, and the **prefrontal cortex**, involved in the regulation of behavior. A **Western blot**, an antibody-based assay, separated FosB-family proteins by molecular mass. Cocaine-dependent subjects had lower hippocampal ΔFosB and other FosB-family signals. The sampled prefrontal cortex did not exhibit the same group differences. The direction of the finding depended on the sampled region.

The hippocampal samples also had reduced **GluA2**, a subunit of AMPA-type receptors that carry excitatory glutamate signals. Reduced GluA2 accompanied the altered FosB-family pattern. Human and mouse samples shared major FosB-family protein bands, while human tissue contained additional detected bands. Mouse controls tested whether delayed collection explained those additional signals. Regional sampling and protein identification shape comparisons between animal mechanisms and human observations.

Gajewski PA, Turecki G, Robison AJ. (2016). Differential Expression of FosB Proteins and Potential Target Genes in Select Brain Regions of Addiction and Depression Patients. PloS one 11:e0160355. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0160355
