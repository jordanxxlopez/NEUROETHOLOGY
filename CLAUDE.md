# NEUR 411 Neuroethology — Fall 2026

This repo builds the course's lecture PowerPoints.

- **To make any lecture, use the `neuroethology-lecture` skill** (`.claude/skills/neuroethology-lecture/SKILL.md`). It holds every format rule; follow it without waiting to be reminded.
- Lectures 8, 9 and 10 are the approved model (46 slides: title + 44 content + key takeaways). Lectures 1-7 are an older format — do not copy them. Their closing "does not establish" caveat paragraphs predate the Oct 9 rule; do not copy that habit.
- **Never create images.** Figures come from published articles (cropped panels, `Author (year), Fig. N`, DOI) — these get priority. Nearly every slide carries an image: at least 40 of 44 content slides, at least 34 with primary-article figures. The title slide shows the study animal (paper figure or credited web photo). Any slide about a specific structure (brain region, neuron, sense organ) carries an anatomy image of it. Credited web images (`Photo: …`, credit, license; neuroscience-related is fine) only where needed, max 10. Prefer colorful figures (fluorescence/stained micrographs, color maps and plots, color animal photos) over grayscale — color on at least half of the image slides, color only from the source, never recolored; backgrounds unchanged. No schematics, diagrams, re-plotted charts, illustrations or AI images. If a PDF can't be obtained, ask the instructor to upload it; otherwise use a text slide. **Exception — Unit 4 special topics (lectures 41+, date "Date TBD", listed under `special_topics` in `course/schedule.json`):** don't stop for PDFs; use what is accessible, swap in another accessible primary paper when one is blocked, and ask only if a truly foundational paper is unavailable.
- **Useful article videos:** apply to new lectures and existing decks being updated. Check supplements and article-provided recordings; embed useful original media in the PPTX when supported, with author/year, exact movie identifier or filename, DOI, original URL, credit and supplied license. Sources must be publisher/article media or an author/lab repository explicitly linked or cited by the article. **Never YouTube**, unrelated web videos, generated animation or reconstructed footage. Compatibility conversion to MP4 is allowed without changing scene, timing, audio or scientific content. Verify saved media and playback when possible, retain a credited static fallback, and distinguish static exports from the complete embedded-video PPTX. If unavailable, explain briefly, provide the original link/file where possible and continue. Aim for 8–10 useful embedded original article recordings per lecture (10 preferred, 8 acceptable); explain any shortfall and never pad with repeated, unrelated or unhelpful clips. Preserve titles, slide counts and figure requirements. Full rule 11 in AGENTS.md and the skill.
- **Writing:** every sentence teaches source content — no roadmap/transition/figure-reading/takeaway framing, no 'this shows/illustrates', no attention-directing phrases, no filler; speaker notes are a natural teaching transcript; concepts over statistics (no p-values, test names, ± errors); no math the sources don't show. Every paragraph teaches; a limitation gets a sentence only when it changes what students should conclude, not on every slide (no closing "does not establish" paragraphs). No fixed paragraph structure. No em dashes in slide text. Full rules in the skill; enforced by `tools/style_rules.py`.
- Lecture titles and dates live in `course/schedule.json`. Never change a title. Special topics (Unit 4, lectures 41+) go under `special_topics`, titled in the same style, date "Date TBD"; they replace a scheduled lecture only when the instructor chooses.
- Color themes: `course/themes.json` — never reuse one, no yellow/orange, no purple/green. Titles and bold text are a darker shade of the title-slide color; all other text is black (`tools/theme_colors.py`, enforced by the checker).
- Tools: `tools/theme_colors.py` (re-align a finished deck's colors), `tools/build_lecture.py` (spec → .pptx, then checks), `tools/check_lecture.py` (rule checker for any deck), `tools/crop_figure.py` (cut figure panels from paper PDFs).
- Python deps: `pip install -r requirements.txt` (python-pptx, Pillow) and poppler (`pdftoppm`).
- `AGENTS.md` holds the same rules for Codex; keep it in sync with the skill.


- **First-slide images must be unique.** Every image on slide 1 must show the animal studied in that lecture. Use an original experimental image or a credited real photograph chosen for appearance, subject to the existing source and license rules. Never reuse an image already used anywhere in an earlier lecture; another crop, resize, or encoding of the same image does not make it new. Before choosing it, compare the candidate with images in previous lecture decks and record its source and the uniqueness check. This no-repeat requirement applies only to slide 1; content-slide figures may recur when scientifically appropriate.

## Advisory Board (standing review panel)

Jordan's personal board of advisors is a general-purpose panel that scrutinizes any work, decision or plan before it is delivered. Each member reviews from a fixed angle, calls out anything that breaks Jordan's rules or standards, and problems are fixed and re-checked.

**Members (fixed roles, each with a name Jordan can call on directly):**
- **Cato, Inspector General**: guards the board itself against corruption of the process. Does NOT review the work's content (not an honesty or ethics checker). Watches for: any member or the Chair going against Jordan's rules or policies, skewed or rigged votes and sign-offs, rubber-stamping, a member being steamrolled or ignored, issues quietly dropped or softened, the Chair overriding objections, the board drifting from Jordan's standards or deciding something that is Jordan's to decide. Usually silent. Has stop power: if the process is compromised, review halts and the problem is reported to Jordan until resolved. Minor concerns are flagged and the review continues.
- **Margaret, Chair**: runs the session, sets what rules and standards apply to this task (from memory, past sessions, approved examples), breaks ties, gives final sign-off.
- **Gideon, Policy Enforcer**: checks the work against Jordan's explicit rules, banned patterns, required structure and style. Calls out each violation with location, rule broken and fix.
- **Vera, Fact-Checker**: checks that everything is factual and not incorrect, false, fabricated or misleading, including statements that are technically true but give the wrong impression. Anything wrong gets fixed. Source-vs-truth rule: when a source (e.g. a professor) taught or did something incorrectly or differently from the accepted facts, the work records faithfully what the source said or did (it is what Jordan is tested on), and Vera does NOT silently correct it or silently repeat it. Vera flags the discrepancy to Jordan in the board report with the correct version, kept out of the deliverable itself unless Jordan says to include it. The deliverable should never state something false as if it were true, and should never misrepresent what the source actually said.
- **Sage, Expert/Scientist**: checks depth and domain quality: right terminology, completeness, nuance, whether it reflects how an expert in the field would put it. As the resident expert/scientist, Sage also checks truth and may flag or fix factual errors. Sage and Vera both fact-check, from different angles (Sage from domain expertise, Vera as the dedicated line-by-line fact-checker); if they disagree, Margaret resolves it and Cato checks the resolution wasn't skewed.
- **Rook, Devil's Advocate**: argues against the work. Finds the weakest point, hidden assumptions, and the strongest objection someone could raise.
- **Flint, Realist**: checks feasibility and fit with real constraints (time, length, effort, audience). Cuts what doesn't earn its place.
- **Ada, Strategist**: checks that the work serves Jordan's actual goal, that priorities are right, and what the longer-term or next move should be.

**How a session works:**
1. Chair states the goal and the rules in force.
2. Each member reviews from their angle and logs issues as: member, location, problem, rule or reason, fix.
3. Issues get fixed; changed parts are re-reviewed until nobody objects.
4. Margaret (Chair) signs off with a brief report: what was caught, what was fixed, anything unresolved. Cato can halt the process at any point before that.

**Scope and scale:** Roles can be added, renamed or dropped at Jordan's direction. Full board for substantial work, a lighter pass for small tasks. Do not run the board on anything unless the work is being delivered or Jordan asks for it.
